# Master section checklist

Work through this list for every agreement and include each item that is
relevant to the deal. Not every deal needs every section, but you must
consciously decide, not forget. Exact titles adapt to the deal. Number the
clauses with a single flat running integer sequence (1, 2, 3 through to the end
of the agreement), never decimal numbering such as 1.1 or 2.3; sub items inside
a clause take Hebrew letters (א), (ב), (ג). See `rtl-docx.md`.

## Front matter
- Title that names the deal type accurately (for example הסכם שיתוף פעולה, ייזום וחלוקת רווחים).
- Date and place line.
- Parties block. Name each party with full identifying details and capacity.
  Where one signature binds another party, state it. Where parties are jointly
  and severally liable, say ביחד ולחוד. Where a party signs personally rather
  than through an entity, make the personal liability explicit.
- Recitals (הואיל) that tell the factual and commercial story: the asset, the
  intent, the professional and regulatory background, the parties' roles, and
  the desire to regulate the relationship fully.
- An operative lead in (לפיכך הוצהר, הותנה והוסכם).

## Core body
1. Introduction, appendices, interpretation, headings.
2. Definitions. Define every recurring term, especially the money terms.
3. Representations and warranties of the parties, including a condition to
   verify identity and title where the registry is unclear.
4. Disclosure of impediments in good faith, where relevant. Failure to disclose
   a known impediment in good faith can be defined as a fundamental breach; this
   is a strong protection for the party relying on the other's disclosures.
5. Purpose of the engagement.
6. Description of the asset and the project.
7. Term and exclusivity.
8. Standing of the acting party and powers. If no power of attorney is given,
   state that authority rests on a contractual declaration within the agreement.
9. Promotion of the planning or regulatory process, with deadlines.
10. Any land or rights to be annexed, and any authority share or requirement.
11. Resolution of existing encumbrances, third party rights, and roof or
    airspace rights where a project depends on them.
12. Cooperation obligations of the owner or passive party.
13. Investment and financing. Who funds what, any cap, when responsibility ends.
14. The purchase or exit party (a fund or buyer), and who is and is not liable
    for the price.
15. Consideration. Amount, whether plus tax, who pays it, and by when.
16. Return of investment. From the waterfall, and directly on the other side's breach.
17. Profit distribution. The split, and what is deducted before it (tax, investment).
18. Adjustment or dilution mechanism. Prefer an event based trigger with a
    relative formula and a worked example. State whether it touches the fixed
    consideration and whether it is the sole such mechanism.
19. Right of first refusal and third party sale waterfall, if any.
20. Tax. Plus tax on the price, incidence of statutory taxes, and how tax feeds
    the profit computation.
21. Confidentiality.
22. Non compete.
23. Intellectual property in the plans and professional materials.
24. Assignment.
25. Insurance.
26. Liability and indemnity. Note that indemnity for proven damage is in
    addition to any liquidated damages.
27. Breach, cure periods, remedies, and liquidated damages. See below.
28. Force majeure, regulatory and planning changes, war, regulatory delay.
29. Insolvency, change of control, death of a party.
30. Termination and separation mechanics.
31. Dispute resolution (for example confidential arbitration).
32. Governing law and jurisdiction.
33. Notices.
34. General (entire agreement, amendments in writing, no waiver, severability,
    no partnership unless expressly agreed).
35. Signatures, with the appropriate blocks and lawyer authentication.

## Liquidated damages clause
When the client wants a fixed penalty without proof of damage, draft it under
section 15 of the Remedies Law (חוק החוזים (תרופות בשל הפרת חוזה), התשל"א 1970):
- A fixed sum per fundamental breach, payable without proof of damage.
- A recital that the sum is a reasonable and proportionate pre estimate of the
  expected damage, assessed at signing. This supports enforceability and reduces
  the risk of judicial reduction for a disproportionate penalty.
- State clearly whether it is a ceiling and sole remedy or in addition to all
  other remedies. In addition to is the stronger, client protective form: it
  stacks on enforcement, rescission, return of investment, and full indemnity
  for proven damage beyond the agreed sum.
- Consider tiering the sum by breach type if the client wants that.
